# Commercial validation and opportunity assessment

October 25, 2026. Hypotheses, not a forecast or investment pitch.

## Evidence to date

The archived five-brand study compared ten preselected partners in two domains with singleton and equal-rank blend controls. Seventeen of twenty comparisons cleared a descriptive distance cutoff from both singletons, but the median distance to a simple blend was only about .016. These are cultural association results, not proof of synergy, loyalty or partnership effectiveness. No independent participant outcomes established incremental utility. See [findings](../research/civilizr-research/whole-personality/WHOLE_PERSONALITY_FINDINGS.md).

The game now offers an optional comparison between model influence and self-report. We have validated software behavior with fixtures and browser interactions, not the commercial hypothesis with people. A strong recommendation influence can overshadow a brand; the biggest hijacker is not automatically its best partner.

## Small proposed pilot

Recruit one real brand as a consenting research collaborator. Concrete candidate: Blue Bottle Coffee, already represented in the archived study; there is no agreement or expressed interest from Blue Bottle. Defined audience: consenting adult specialty-coffee buyers in one market, including current and occasional Blue Bottle customers. Decision: which of three feasible local creator/community collaborations merits a small activation test. The brand supplies an affordable, rights-cleared shortlist, equal-budget offer and actual constraints. Do not imply endorsement or launch public branded materials without its participation.

Stage 1: 30–50 consenting participants for feasibility and effect-size estimation, not definitive lift. Collect identity attachment before exposing model results; include a short repeat measure a week later. Keep research enrollment and contact scheduling outside the public game's anonymous export, with separate consent and a deletion schedule. The partner retains customer/contact data and provides only consented study IDs/outcomes. Establish feasibility of this linkage before promising behavioral validation.

Have a strategist rank the same candidates using an explicit audience brief, existing customer research and reach/cost information. Separately rank using Qloo full/partner/singleton/blend comparisons plus the same brief. Blind participants and evaluating strategists to method labels; randomize order; hold creative quality, offer and exposure constant. If methods choose the same candidate, report no discriminating decision rather than manufacture a difference. Record rationale, decision changes, time spent and repeat-use interest; these are product-utility outcomes, not willingness to pay.

Stage 2: only if feasibility and permissions hold, randomize a brand-approved limited activation among baseline-chosen partner, Qloo-assisted partner, and equivalent brand-only control. Predefine primary behavior (e.g. verified event attendance or offer redemption per assigned participant within 14 days), report intention-to-treat denominators and uncertainty. Set a minimum useful difference from the brand's actual activation costs before seeing results. Use the pilot variance/base rate to size a subsequent trial; 30–50 people cannot reliably establish modest commercial lift.

Measure actual repeat purchase at 30/60 days only with separate consent and partner transaction verification. Loyalty/preference survey, advocacy intention and willingness to attend are stated outcomes. Redemption, repeat purchases, verified referrals and community attendance are behaviors. Do not present clicks or stated intent as retention. Include a standardized preference choice against alternatives to distinguish general liking from brand-specific preference.

Identity transfer requires pre/post brand attachment, creator/community attachment measured before treatment, randomized exposure and a brand-only control, with durability and observed behavior. An interaction between prior partner attachment and treatment benefit would support the mechanism; it still needs replication beyond one audience. Control for pre-existing brand affinity, offer value and exposure, and test whether the partner distracts from brand recall. Study association first; causal transfer is a higher bar.

## Three possible businesses

| | A. Consumer cultural identity game | B. Identity Intelligence | C. Brand Partnership Intelligence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Customer / problem | Players seeking entertaining self-expression; potentially sponsors seeking participation | Consumer insight teams needing to understand gaps between inferred taste and expressed importance | Brand/creator partnership teams deciding where to spend limited activation budgets |
| Existing support | Working adaptive game, grounded receipts, share images and optional confrontation | Typed comparisons, honest missingness and minimal opt-in export now exist | Controlled five-brand prototype shows contextual variation |
| Unvalidated | Voluntary sharing, repeat play, acquisition economics and sponsor demand | Reliability, representativeness, behavioral prediction and incremental decision value | Discovery quality, partnership feasibility, attachment transfer, lift and buyer demand |
| Possible revenue | Optional paid creative formats or clearly disclosed sponsored experiences, subject to rights; no assumed demand | Scoped research engagements, then repeat studies if clients use findings | Paid shortlist/experiment engagements; software subscription only after repeated workflows justify it |
| Competitors / substitutes | Personality quizzes, recommendation demos, social sharing formats and Qloo's own taste examples | Surveys, interviews, customer panels, social listening and first-party customer research | Agencies, creator marketplaces, audience/reach tools, strategist judgment and Qloo itself |
| Qloo dependence | High for grounded recommendations/removal evidence; entertainment alone could use other inputs | High for current model-comparison arm; self-report and behavioral validation can be provider-independent | High for cultural-fit component; economics, reach, logistics and observed lift require other evidence |
| Potential proprietary advantage | Distinctive experience and distribution, if users return | Consented, longitudinal, externally validated data linked to useful outcomes; responses alone are not a moat | A validated decision/experiment workflow and repeat outcome evidence, if transferable across clients |
| Next experiment | Observe 10–15 adult testers; time the optional step, test comprehension, voluntary share and skip paths | Independently collect pre-reveal attachment and retest; check stability and added predictive value beyond ordinary surveys | Conduct the blinded brand pilot above; establish a decision improvement over a credible baseline |

Do not infer a market size, price, demand or moat from a working demo. Convenience samples from a viral game are selected for culture engagement and willingness to share. Consent selection, repeat players, provider coverage, recommendation-selected panels and post-reveal anchoring further limit inference. Independent recruitment, sampling weights where justified, holdout validation and permission for derived data use would be prerequisites for stronger claims.

## Hosting and provider review

The app is a local single-process Python prototype. It has no public deployment or commercial data store. Research export is appropriate at this stage; an enterprise dashboard would conceal unvalidated measurement behind a product interface.

Public sources checked October 25, 2026; account-specific contracts were unavailable:

- [Qloo terms](https://www.qloo.com/legal/terms): negotiated agreements may supersede public terms; restrictions on redistribution/charging and rights in outputs require account-specific confirmation for public display, exports, paid embedding and derived datasets. Do not assume participant consent also supplies vendor rights.
- [NVIDIA API trial terms](https://assets.ngc.nvidia.com/products/api-catalog/legal/NVIDIA%20API%20Trial%20Terms%20of%20Service.pdf): trial access is for limited testing/evaluation; production requires an appropriate subscription/agreement. Hackathon/public demonstration permissions must be checked against the applicable program terms before external release.
- [Qloo capabilities](https://www.qloo.com/capabilities): combination-based cultural interpretation and cross-domain recommendations overlap the proposed commercial product. The workflow and independently validated outcomes would need to supply additional value.

These source checks are scope boundaries, not a determination of this account's negotiated rights. No automatic data submission or deployment was added.

## Three highest-impact steps before the hackathon deadline

1. Observe 10–15 consenting adult players on their own phones: verify the optional step takes roughly 15–30 seconds, people understand the model/self-report distinction, and sharing still works when skipped. Record observed completion and actual voluntary share separately from intentions.
2. Confirm public-demo/provider rights, configure HTTPS hosting with API quotas and abuse limits, and test real iOS/Android downloads. Maintain a recorded fallback and one repeatable live feedback-driven agent demonstration.
3. Run a small independent pre-reveal identity study with a strategist reviewing the resulting evidence; recruit one real brand for the scoped pilot rather than building the enterprise interface. Publish null or ambiguous findings. No hackathon date was supplied, so this is priority order rather than an invented schedule.
