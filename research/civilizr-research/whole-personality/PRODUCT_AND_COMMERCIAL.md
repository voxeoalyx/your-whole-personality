# Product hypotheses and commercial constraints

2026-10-08. Proposed validation only; no product has been built.

**Your Whole Personality — consumer game.** A person supplies three supported interests, sees which removal changes two recommendation domains most, and swaps one interest to challenge the result. Show named recommendations and a sensitivity explanation, including “no clear winner.” Ask which suggestions they actually like before offering a shareable result. The technical mechanic is supported by this experiment; entertainment, retention and sharing are untested. Never display a percentage of human personality. The next deliverable should be a moderated test, not production infrastructure.

**Identity Stress Test — brand strategy.** A conditional research workflow could compare a brand, two proposed partners, both partner-only controls, and a simple rank-blend baseline. A creative strategist or partnership planner with a concrete shortlist is the clearest plausible buyer: they need hypotheses and explanations that can be checked against their brief. A scoped research engagement is a more testable business model than assuming a recurring SaaS subscription. Validate time saved, genuinely new actionable hypotheses and willingness to commission a second study. Only later investigate seats or usage pricing; no pricing, TAM or revenue estimates are justified here.

**Cultural White Space — brand discovery.** Not supported as a distinct product yet. An adjacent affinity is a discovery lead. Calling it white space requires competitor presence, current activations, audience demand and constraints outside Qloo. Without these, it largely duplicates ordinary recommendations. Low affinity alone is not untapped compatibility.

Qloo itself already provides combination-based cultural inference and an interactive taste demonstration. Its marketer materials position its data for creative ideas, media plans and partnership choices. That overlaps both the consumer display and commercial premise; our incremental contribution is the explicit ablation/control workflow, uncertainty disclosure and eventually validation data. It is an implementation difference, not an established moat. Old report/data-licensing URLs redirected during this review, so current capabilities and the marketing use case are the supporting references. [Capabilities](https://www.qloo.com/capabilities), [marketing and media planning](https://www.qloo.com/use-cases/plan-marketing-and-media-campaigns).

**Commercial permission is not established.** Qloo's public terms, last updated December 19, 2024 and reviewed October 8, 2026, allow a negotiated agreement to supersede them. Section 5 restricts resale, redistribution, sublicensing and charging for access to Services; section 8 asserts broad rights over outputs and derivatives; section 9 grants Qloo broad rights over submitted anonymized input signals. Account-specific agreements were unavailable. Before commercial use, obtain written confirmation covering paid embedding, public display, caching, derived scores, exports, end-user access, attribution, and ownership/use of collected feedback. The public terms alone do not validate the proposed license model. [Qloo terms](https://www.qloo.com/legal/terms).

A differentiated dataset would require consented interest combinations, withheld preferences, corrections, repeated ratings and decisions/outcomes, with usable rights. Keep raw API-derived data conceptually separate from first-party feedback; neither is automatically proprietary. Competitors can reproduce our sensitivity arithmetic with the same API. The plausible moat would be accumulated evaluation evidence, distribution and a trusted workflow, if those materialize.

Before selling decision intelligence: test new brands and independent candidate panels; replicate across time; compare against an LLM and the singleton blend; have independent strategists evaluate shortlists blind; then prospectively measure whether hypotheses improve an actual campaign or research decision with appropriate controls. A modeled combination is not an intervention on a brand. Until those tests succeed, sell neither predicted brand lift nor sales outcomes. The current result supports a consumer-first validation experiment, with commercial research kept conditional.
