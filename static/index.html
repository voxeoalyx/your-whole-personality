The joke is on
the algorithm.

The live investigator uses Qloo evidence and your answers to choose up to five predictions. NVIDIA hosts the model that directs the investigation. Recorded rounds use a fixed six-guess sequence. We also remove each interest, one at a time, and see how the same shortlists move.

“Predictability” means confirmations among familiar guesses. “Haven’t tried it” is not a wrong answer. Ranking sensitivity is separate: a winner needs a clear lead in two domains and agreement across both halves of the shortlists.

These are small, model-selected lists. Neither measurement says how interesting you are, who you are, or what you will buy. A tie or incomplete response means we do not name a winner.

Your privacy: live interests and cultural evidence are sent to Qloo and NVIDIA; live reactions go to this server and NVIDIA to select the next test. Explicitly liked predictions may become additional Qloo inputs. Server caches last up to two hours; investigation state expires after 30 minutes and is lost on restart. Reload starts a new UI; the old server round expires normally. Provider data policies apply. Recorded-round reactions remain in your tab. No accounts, analytics or advertising profiles. Images load from the URLs Qloo supplies. Exporting a card or session is your choice.

Recorded rounds use real, saved Qloo responses for an example profile. They are always labeled. There are no public result links in this local prototype.
